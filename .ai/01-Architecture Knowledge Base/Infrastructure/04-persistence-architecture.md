# Persistence Architecture

## Purpose

This document defines the architectural approach used to persist business data in Mawthooq.

The persistence architecture is designed to provide explicit control over database operations while preserving the isolation of the business model from implementation details.

Persistence is considered a technical concern owned entirely by the Infrastructure layer.

---

## Objectives

The persistence architecture is designed to achieve the following goals:

- Preserve domain purity.
- Maintain explicit control over database operations.
- Ensure transactional consistency.
- Support predictable performance.
- Isolate database technology from the business model.
- Keep persistence behavior deterministic.

---

## Architectural Ownership

Persistence belongs exclusively to the Infrastructure layer.

Responsibilities include:

- Aggregate persistence.
- Database communication.
- SQL execution.
- Transaction management.
- Concurrency control.
- Repository implementations.

Business decisions remain outside the persistence layer.

---

## Aggregate Persistence

Aggregates are the unit of persistence.

Each repository persists exactly one aggregate root and is responsible for maintaining the consistency of its aggregate.

Repositories never coordinate multiple aggregates.

Cross-aggregate workflows are coordinated by the Application layer.

---

## Repository Responsibility

Repositories provide the persistence implementation required by the Application layer.

Their responsibilities include:

- Loading aggregates.
- Persisting aggregate state.
- Deleting aggregate data when applicable.
- Applying concurrency checks.
- Translating persistence failures into infrastructure-level exceptions where appropriate.

Repositories do not implement business rules.

---

## Persistence Strategy

Persistence operations are explicit.

The Infrastructure layer determines how aggregate changes are translated into database operations while honoring the business state produced by the Domain layer.

No implicit persistence behavior is assumed.

---

## Transaction Ownership

Transactions define the consistency boundary of a business operation.

The Application layer decides when a transaction is required.

The Infrastructure layer executes and manages the transaction.

Repositories participate in the active transaction but do not create transaction boundaries themselves.

---

## Consistency

A successful business operation must leave the persistent state consistent.

Either all required persistence operations succeed, or none of them are committed.

Partial persistence is not considered a valid outcome.

---

## Concurrency

Persistence uses optimistic concurrency to protect business data from conflicting modifications.

Concurrency validation is performed during persistence rather than during business execution.

A concurrency conflict causes the current persistence operation to fail without partially applying changes.

---

## SQL Ownership

SQL is an implementation detail of the Infrastructure layer.

Business layers remain unaware of:

- SQL statements
- Tables
- Schemas
- Database-specific features

Only repositories and supporting infrastructure components interact directly with SQL.

---

## Technology Independence

The business architecture does not depend on any persistence technology.

Database engines, drivers, and data access libraries are considered replaceable implementation choices.

Changing persistence technology should not require modifications to Domain or Application logic.

---

## Error Handling

Persistence failures are treated as technical failures.

Infrastructure is responsible for detecting and reporting these failures while preserving the consistency of the business operation.

Business validation errors are never generated by the persistence layer.

---

## Design Principles

The persistence architecture follows these principles:

- Explicit persistence.
- Aggregate-oriented design.
- Controlled transactions.
- Predictable behavior.
- Technology isolation.
- Operational reliability.

These principles guide every persistence implementation within Mawthooq.

---

## Out of Scope

The persistence architecture does not define:

- Database schema design.
- SQL organization.
- Connection management.
- Dependency injection.
- Logging.
- External integrations.

These concerns are documented separately within the Infrastructure Knowledge Base.
